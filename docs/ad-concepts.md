# Ad concepts for the fast-track homepage

Landing page: https://velocitystud.io (UTM every link; the page stores utm_* and passes them to Pipedrive and Calendly).
Audience: California homeowners 35 to 65 who already know they want an ADU, a remodel, or a simple new home, and who have been quoted or ghosted by a slow drafter. Not developers, not plan aggregators.
Rule for every ad: a specific number in the first second, the mechanism or the licensed human in the next two, one boundary so it reads as honest, then the two-minute qualify.

Formats to produce for each concept: 4:5 (feed), 9:16 (Reels and Stories), 1:1 (secondary). Text on image stays inside the center 80% so Reels UI does not cover it.

Each concept tests one variable. Keep the body copy identical across concepts where the hook is the variable, and vice versa.

## V1 · "Days, not months" (video, 20 to 30 s) — the control

Hook (first 2 s, on screen): **Permit-ready plans in 5 business days.**
Beats:
1. 0-2 s: the hero checkerboard scrolling, text card over it: "Permit-ready plans in 5 business days."
2. 2-8 s: the site's solution clip (shared-screen 3D model with the chat bubbles). Text: "You sketch it with us live. We lock it. Every discipline draws at once."
3. 8-16 s: three sheet tiles slide in one after another (site plan, floor plan, elevations). Text: "Stamped by a California licensed architect. Structural, MEP, Title 24 included."
4. 16-22 s: the spectrum bar from the hero animates from "500 sq ft ADU · $6,000" to "4,000 sq ft house · $20,000".
5. 22-28 s: "Most California lots qualify. Steep lots and fire zones are fine. HOA or coastal review first? We'll tell you on the call." Button: "See if your project qualifies."
Primary text: "ADU, remodel, or simple new home: a stamped, permit-ready set in 5 to 10 business days, priced by size from $6,000. Two minutes to see if your lot qualifies."
Headline: "Permit plans in days, not months."
Assets: all exist (hero tiles, solution-call.mp4, spectrum bar). Needs a 20-30 s edit and a voice or captions. No new generation.

## V2 · "Still waiting on your drafter?" (video, 15 s) — the pain hook

Hook: **Still waiting on your drafter?**
Beats:
1. 0-5 s: the pain clip (homeowner across the desk, architect slowly working). Text: "Days for a reply. Weeks for drawings. Months for a permit."
2. 5-10 s: hard cut to the solution clip. Text: "One architect on your project, and only yours, until it is out the door."
3. 10-15 s: "5 business days for an ADU. 10 for a house. From $6,000." Button.
Primary text: "If your architect takes days to reply and weeks per round of drawings, you are paying for their other projects. We put one production architect on yours and deliver a stamped set in 5 to 10 business days."
Headline: "Your plans, on the date we promised."
Assets: pain-desk.mp4 and solution-call.mp4 exist. Needs a 9:16 recrop of the solution clip (it is 16:10); regenerate with Veo at 9:16 from the same still, or crop in the edit.
Tests: pain-first versus promise-first opening (against V1).

## V3 · Richman to camera (video, 30 to 40 s) — the trust hook

Hook: **"I'm a California licensed architect, and I stamp every set we deliver."**
Beats (per the scaffold's video spec):
1. 0-3 s: Richman on camera, phone or DSLR, daylight, the studio behind him. "If you need ADU or house plans and you need them this month, watch this."
2. 3-10 s: text card with the promise and the price.
3. 10-25 s: screen recording of the real workflow: parcel lookup, live layout sketch, the model updating, sheets filling in. Real screens, not stock.
4. 25-35 s: the honest boundary, spoken: "Flat lot, standard city, you know what you want: we're the fastest option in California. Hillside or coastal: we'll tell you on the call."
5. 35-40 s: "Two minutes to see if you qualify." URL.
Primary text: "Most architects deliver permit sets three to five times slower than they should. We built a firm around not doing that. Here is how it works."
Headline: "A licensed architect. Five to ten business days."
Assets: needs a real recording of Richman (two takes, 60 s each, phone at eye level in window light) and a 15 s screen capture of the actual tools. Nothing generated; this is the proof ad and must be real.

## I1 · Price-and-days ladder (image set)

Visual: the hero spectrum bar rebuilt as a static graphic on the dark hero background, two end labels, three small tiles under it (small ADU rendering, house sheet, large house rendering). One version per end of the range so each can be its own ad: an ADU version, a house version.
Primary text: "Flat prices by size. Days by size. Stamped by a California licensed architect, structural, MEP, and Title 24 included. See where your project lands."
Headline: "Fixed-fee permits, $6K to $20K."
Assets: all exist. Design work only.
Tests: price-forward versus time-forward (against I2).

## I2 · "The city gives you 60 days. We need 5." (image, text-led)

Hook: **The city takes 60 days to review an ADU. We take 5 to draw it.**
Visual: plain, high contrast, two lines of large type on the forest green, a single stamped-sheet tile in the corner. The least designed ad in the set on purpose.
Primary text: "State law gives the city 60 days on an ADU. Most drafters take longer than that just to hand you the set. We deliver a stamped, permit-ready set in 5 business days, then answer every plan check comment within 3 business days until the permit issues."
Headline: "Permit-ready in 5 business days."
Assets: one sheet tile (exists).
Tests: a contrast claim against a direct claim (I1).

## I3 · The sheet set (image carousel, 5 cards)

Hook: **Everything the city needs, in one stamped set.**
Cards: site plan, floor plan, elevations, structural framing, Title 24 and MEP, each a hero sheet tile with a one-line caption; final card the included-list cover sheet from the site and the button.
Primary text: "Cover and notes, site plan, floor and roof plans, elevations, sections, structural plans and calcs, plumbing, electrical, mechanical, Title 24. Drawn in parallel by one team and stamped by a California licensed architect. Five to ten business days."
Headline: "What's included in the permit package."
Assets: all exist (hero sheet tiles, cover-sheet section). Carousel assembly only.
Tests: completeness as the hook, for the buyer whose fear is "will the city accept it".

## I4 · Pre-approved plan angle (image, ADU only)

Hook: **The city's free ADU plan doesn't fit your lot. Ours will, in 5 days.**
Visual: split: left a generic pre-approved plan outline not fitting a lot shape; right one of our site-plan sheets with the ADU placed inside the setbacks.
Primary text: "Pre-approved plans are free until you need a site plan, an energy report, structural adaptation, and someone to carry it through plan check. We start from a layout that fits your lot and finish all of that in the same five days."
Headline: "Fits your lot. Permit-ready in 5 days."
Assets: needs one new composite from an existing site-plan sheet. Small design job.
Tests: a competitor-substitute angle against our own-merits angles.

## Measurement

- UTMs: utm_source=meta, utm_medium=paid, utm_campaign=fasttrack-{week}, utm_content={concept id}, e.g. V2-9x16. The page carries these into the Pipedrive person and the Calendly booking.
- The qualify form fires Meta "Lead" with the score; a pass is tier high, a fail is tier mid. Optimize the campaign toward the high tier via the CAPI deal-stage events, not toward form submits, so Meta learns the ready buyer.
- Judge a concept on qualified leads (passes) per dollar, not clicks. The pass rule is on the page, so a concept that attracts "not sure" timing answers is the wrong audience even if it is cheap.

## Production notes

- Site clips are 8 s loops at 16:10 and 4:5. For 9:16 ads, regenerate from the same stills with Veo at 9:16 rather than cropping, using the same motion prompts in homepage-image-prompts.md.
- Keep every claim consistent with the page: 5 business days is the small ADU, 10 is the large house, days and prices scale by size in between. Do not say "5 days" for a house.
- No license number in ads; "California licensed architect" is the claim.
- The judge script (scripts/judge-images.py) works on ad stills too if more renderings are generated.
